# Week 1 · Topic 3 Study Guide — Controls

Controls carry about 19 of the 60 marks in an average Test 1, and the range is wide: B23F awarded
24 marks to controls, 16 of them to tests of controls alone.

## What you will be able to do this week

- Tell a control, a weakness, a test of control and a substantive procedure apart from the verb in the stem.
- Identify the controls an auditor could rely on, and state the objective each achieves (validity, accuracy, completeness).
- Write a test of control that names the action verb, the document, the sample and the period.

<div class="lesson-callout" markdown="1">
**The single most expensive error on the paper:** answering a tests-of-control question with
substantive procedures. Does your sentence check whether the CONTROL operated, or whether the BALANCE is right?
</div>

## 1. The four words students confuse

Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=REPLACE_WITH_RECORDING_ID

| Term | What it is | Who does it |
|---|---|---|
| A control | Something in the client's system that prevents or detects error or fraud | The client |
| A weakness | Something missing or defective in that system | Nobody — that is the point |
| A test of control | A procedure that checks whether the control operated effectively | The auditor |
| A substantive procedure | A procedure that tests the balance itself | The auditor |

## 2. Tests of controls — ISA 330

A test of control names **what** the auditor does (inspect, re-perform, observe, enquire), **to which
document**, for **a sample** across **the period**:

> For a sample of picking slips throughout the year, INSPECT the slip for Mr Zulu's signature.

## Working this topic
